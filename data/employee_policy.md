# Employee Policy Handbook

**Document Owner:** People Operations  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective Date:** 1 January 2026  
**Review Cycle:** Annual

This handbook describes the company's standard employee policies and operating expectations. Unless a policy explicitly states otherwise, employees should contact People Operations or their manager for clarification.

---

# 1. Leave and Time Off

## 1.1 Annual Leave

Full-time employees receive **24 working days of annual leave per calendar year**.

Annual leave should normally be requested at least **5 working days in advance** through the company's leave-management system. Managers may approve shorter-notice requests when business requirements permit.

Annual leave is intended for planned personal time off. Employees should coordinate extended leave with their manager so that critical work and operational responsibilities remain covered.

Unused annual leave may be carried forward for up to **12 working days** into the following calendar year. Any balance above the permitted carry-forward limit expires unless a written exception has been approved by People Operations.

## 1.2 Casual Leave

Employees receive **8 working days of casual leave per calendar year**.

Casual leave may be used for short-term personal matters or unforeseen circumstances. When practical, employees should inform their manager before the start of the working day.

Casual leave cannot normally be combined with more than **5 consecutive working days** of annual leave without manager approval.

Unused casual leave expires at the end of the calendar year and is not carried forward.

## 1.3 Sick Leave

Employees receive **12 working days of sick leave per calendar year**.

Employees who are unable to work because of illness should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible.

For sick leave lasting more than **3 consecutive working days**, the company may request appropriate medical documentation.

Unused sick leave is not convertible to annual leave and does not normally carry forward.

## 1.4 Parental Leave

Eligible employees may take parental leave in accordance with applicable law and company policy.

Employees should notify People Operations as early as reasonably possible when planning parental leave. The company will work with the employee to establish a reasonable transition and coverage plan.

## 1.5 Leave Approval

Leave is approved by the employee's designated manager unless the employee's employment terms or local law require a different process.

Submitting a leave request does not mean that the leave is automatically approved. Employees should confirm approval before making commitments that depend on the leave being granted.

During critical operational periods, managers may ask employees to adjust planned leave when necessary for business continuity, subject to applicable law.

# 2. Working Hours and Attendance

## 2.1 Standard Working Hours

The standard working schedule is **Monday through Friday, 9:30 AM to 6:30 PM**, including a **1-hour meal break**.

Some teams may operate different schedules because of customer, operational, or time-zone requirements. Such schedules must be communicated by the relevant manager.

## 2.2 Attendance

Employees are expected to be available and responsive during their agreed working hours.

Repeated unexplained absence, persistent lateness, or failure to maintain required availability may be addressed through the company's performance-management process.

## 2.3 Flexible Working Hours

Employees may request flexible working hours when their role permits it.

Flexible schedules require manager approval and must continue to provide sufficient overlap with the employee's team and required meetings.

## 2.4 Remote Work

Remote work is permitted for eligible roles subject to team and manager approval.

Employees working remotely must maintain a suitable work environment, protect company information, and remain reachable through approved communication channels during agreed working hours.

Remote work is a working arrangement and does not remove the employee's responsibility to meet normal performance, security, attendance, and communication expectations.

---

# 3. Security and Access

## 3.1 Password Policy

Employees must use strong, unique passwords for company accounts.

Passwords must contain at least **12 characters** and should include a combination of letters, numbers, and symbols.

Employees must not reuse company passwords on personal services.

Passwords must not be shared with colleagues, managers, contractors, or support personnel. IT support will never ask an employee to disclose their password.

## 3.2 Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required for company systems that support it.

Employees must register an approved authentication method and must not approve an unexpected MFA request.

If an employee receives an MFA request that they did not initiate, they must deny the request and report it to the security team.

## 3.3 Account Access

Employees may access only systems and data necessary for their assigned responsibilities.

Access requests must be approved through the company's access-management process.

Employees must not attempt to bypass access controls, authentication mechanisms, network restrictions, or other security controls.

## 3.4 Security Incident Reporting

Suspected security incidents must be reported to the security team **within 1 hour of discovery**.

Examples include suspected phishing, accidental disclosure of confidential information, lost company devices, unauthorized account access, malware, or unexpected access to restricted data.

Employees should not investigate beyond what is necessary to preserve information and protect the company unless instructed by the security team.

# 4. Information and Data Protection

## 4.1 Confidential Information

Employees must protect confidential company information from unauthorized access or disclosure.

Confidential information may include source code, customer information, pricing, financial information, business plans, credentials, internal documents, product designs, and non-public operational information.

Confidential information may be shared only with authorized people who require it for legitimate business purposes.

## 4.2 Personal Data

Employees who handle personal data must process it only for legitimate business purposes and in accordance with applicable privacy requirements.

Personal data must not be copied to personal devices, personal cloud storage, or unauthorized applications.

Employees should collect and retain only the information required for the approved business purpose.

## 4.3 Data Retention

Business records must be retained according to the applicable retention schedule.

Employees must not intentionally delete, alter, or conceal records that are subject to a legal hold, investigation, audit, or approved retention requirement.

When information is no longer required and is eligible for deletion, it should be securely deleted using approved procedures.

## 4.4 Data Sharing

Company data may be shared externally only through approved channels and with appropriate authorization.

Employees must verify the recipient before sending confidential or personal information.

Sensitive files should use approved encryption or secure file-transfer mechanisms when required by the company's security procedures.

---

# 5. Acceptable Use of Company Technology

## 5.1 Company Devices

Company-issued laptops, phones, and other devices are provided primarily for business purposes.

Employees must protect company devices from unauthorized use, theft, and physical damage.

Employees must not disable security software, endpoint protection, disk encryption, or device-management controls.

## 5.2 Software Installation

Employees may install software required for their work when it is approved by the company or available through an approved software-management process.

Unapproved software that creates security, licensing, privacy, or operational risks must not be installed on company-managed devices.

## 5.3 Internet and Network Use

Company networks and internet access must be used responsibly and in accordance with applicable law and company policy.

Employees must not use company systems for malicious activity, unauthorized access, distribution of malware, harassment, or activities that create material security or legal risk.

## 5.4 Personal Use

Reasonable personal use of company technology is permitted when it does not interfere with work, consume excessive resources, violate company policy, or create security or legal risks.

Employees should not use company devices for unauthorized commercial activity.

---

# 6. Artificial Intelligence and Generative AI

## 6.1 Approved AI Tools

Employees may use AI tools approved by the company for legitimate business purposes.

Before using an external AI service, employees must verify that the service is approved for the type of information being processed.

## 6.2 Confidential Data and AI

Employees must not submit confidential company information, customer data, credentials, source code, or other restricted information to public AI services unless the service and use case have been explicitly approved.

When an AI service is approved for sensitive information, employees must follow the applicable data-processing and access-control requirements.

## 6.3 Human Review

AI-generated content must be reviewed by an employee before it is used for important business decisions, customer communications, production systems, legal documents, or other high-impact purposes.

Employees remain responsible for the accuracy, security, and appropriateness of work produced with AI assistance.

## 6.4 AI-Generated Code

Code generated by AI must be reviewed, tested, and checked for security and licensing concerns before being incorporated into production systems.

Developers must not assume that AI-generated code is correct merely because it compiles or passes a superficial test.

# 7. Expenses and Business Travel

## 7.1 Business Expenses

Reasonable business expenses may be reimbursed when they are necessary for work and comply with the company's expense policy.

Employees should obtain required approval before making significant or unusual expenditures.

## 7.2 Expense Claims

Expense claims should normally be submitted within **30 days** of the expense date.

Claims must include sufficient documentation, such as receipts, invoices, or other evidence required by Finance.

False, duplicated, or deliberately misleading expense claims are prohibited.

## 7.3 Business Travel

Business travel must be approved by the appropriate manager before booking.

Employees should use company-approved travel providers where available.

Travel expenses must be reasonable and consistent with the applicable travel limits.

## 7.4 Accommodation

Employees travelling for business may claim reasonable accommodation costs within the limits defined by the company's travel policy.

Personal upgrades and additional costs are normally the employee's responsibility unless specifically approved.

---

# 8. Procurement and Vendor Management

## 8.1 Purchasing

Employees must follow the company's procurement process when purchasing goods or services on behalf of the company.

Employees must not split purchases into smaller transactions to avoid approval thresholds.

## 8.2 Vendor Selection

Vendor selection should consider price, quality, security, reliability, legal requirements, and business suitability.

For services involving company or customer data, appropriate security and privacy review must be completed before access is granted.

## 8.3 Conflicts of Interest

Employees must disclose situations in which a personal relationship or financial interest could influence, or appear to influence, a business decision.

Employees must not select a vendor solely because of a personal benefit.

---

# 9. Code of Conduct

## 9.1 Professional Conduct

Employees are expected to treat colleagues, customers, vendors, and other stakeholders professionally and respectfully.

Harassment, discrimination, threats, intimidation, and abusive behavior are not permitted.

## 9.2 Workplace Communication

Employees should communicate respectfully through email, chat, meetings, documentation, and other workplace channels.

Disagreements about technical or business decisions should focus on facts, requirements, risks, and evidence rather than personal criticism.

## 9.3 Conflicts of Interest

Employees must disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest to their manager or People Operations.

Employees must not use their position at the company to obtain improper personal benefits.

## 9.4 Gifts and Hospitality

Employees must not accept gifts or hospitality that could reasonably influence a business decision.

Nominal business courtesies may be accepted when they are infrequent, transparent, and consistent with applicable law and company policy.

Cash gifts must not be accepted from vendors, customers, or business partners.

---

# 10. Performance and Development

## 10.1 Performance Expectations

Employees are expected to understand the responsibilities of their role and work toward agreed objectives.

Managers should provide regular feedback and address significant performance concerns promptly.

## 10.2 Performance Reviews

Formal performance reviews are conducted **twice per year**.

Performance reviews may consider delivery against objectives, quality of work, collaboration, communication, ownership, and role-specific competencies.

## 10.3 Professional Development

Employees are encouraged to develop skills relevant to their role and career goals.

Training requests may require manager approval and may be subject to budget availability.

---

# 11. Employee Records

## 11.1 Personal Information

Employees must provide accurate information required for employment administration.

Employees should notify People Operations when important personal information changes, such as contact details or emergency-contact information.

## 11.2 Access to Records

Employees may request access to employment records where permitted by applicable law and company procedure.

Access to another employee's records is restricted to authorized personnel with a legitimate business need.

---

# 12. Workplace Safety

## 12.1 Safety Responsibilities

Employees must follow applicable workplace health and safety procedures.

Unsafe conditions should be reported promptly to the relevant manager or facilities team.

## 12.2 Emergencies

During an emergency, employees should follow instructions from designated safety personnel and emergency services.

Employees should not put themselves or others at unnecessary risk while attempting to respond to an incident.

---

# 13. Policy Exceptions

Exceptions to company policies require appropriate authorization.

Employees should not assume that a verbal agreement overrides a written policy unless the person providing the exception has the authority to approve it.

Approved exceptions should be documented where practical, including the scope, duration, and approving authority.

---

# 14. Policy Violations

Policy violations may result in corrective action, up to and including termination of employment, depending on the nature and severity of the violation.

The company may investigate suspected violations and may restrict access to systems or information while an investigation is underway.

Employees are expected to cooperate with legitimate internal investigations.

---

# 15. Questions and Policy Updates

Questions about this handbook should be directed to People Operations or the employee's manager.

The company may update policies when business, legal, security, or operational requirements change.

Employees are responsible for following the current version of applicable policies and reviewing official policy updates communicated through company channels.
